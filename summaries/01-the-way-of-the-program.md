# Chapter 1: The way of the program — summary

**Covers:** Downey opens by defining programming as thinking like a computer scientist, combining mathematics, engineering and natural science. He covers running Python, the first program, arithmetic operators, values and types, formal vs natural languages, and the three kinds of debugging (syntax, runtime, semantic).

## Key points
- Core topic: the way of the program in Python 3, taught incrementally with small examples.
- Every chapter ends with a Debugging section (general techniques and Python pitfalls) and a Glossary of terms defined at first use.
- Exercises range from short checks of understanding to substantial projects, most with linked solutions.

## What the reader takes away
- Downey opens by defining programming as thinking like a computer scientist, combining mathematics, engineering and natural science.
- Practical, runnable patterns rather than language trivia: Downey's stated method is short chapters, minimal jargon, and gradual build-up to avoid trap doors.
